Field Service Operations · By Quinita Whitfield

How do you stop jobs
falling through the cracks
in field service?

Jobs fall through the cracks at the handoffs. A lead moves from the phone to the CRM. An estimate moves from the tech to the office. A finished job moves from dispatch to accounting. Every handoff with no owner and no automatic next step is a spot where a job can disappear.

To stop it, trace a few real jobs from first call to paid invoice. Mark every spot where someone had to remember to do something. Give each of those spots an owner, and connect your tools so the next step happens on its own.

01 · The Real Problem

Why new software rarely fixes it

Most growing field service companies have already tried to fix this. They bought a new CRM. They hired an office manager. They moved from spreadsheets to ServiceTitan or Housecall Pro. A few months later, jobs are still slipping.

That's because the cracks usually aren't inside one tool. They're between tools, and between people. Your CRM can be set up perfectly and still miss every lead that came in by text. Your dispatch board can be spotless while finished jobs wait two weeks to get invoiced.

A new tool can't fix a gap nobody has found. So the first step is finding it.

02 · Where Jobs Slip

The five places jobs fall through the cracks

Every job moves through the same five handoffs. Check each one against how your business runs today.

1
Handoff 01
Lead intake
Leads come in from calls, texts, web forms, Google, and homeshows. Missed calls go to voicemail and most people won't leave one. After-hours forms sit until morning. Paper sign-up sheets never get typed in. Any lead that doesn't land in your CRM right away is at risk.
2
Handoff 02
Booking the estimate
The lead is in your system, but getting on the calendar takes three rounds of phone tag. Homeowners usually reach out to more than one company. The one who books first usually gets the visit.
3
Handoff 03
Following up on the estimate
This is the biggest leak for most companies. The bid goes out, and the follow-up lives in someone's head. When that person gets busy, the follow-up never happens. The customer reads silence as "they don't want the job."
4
Handoff 04
Office to field
The schedule is in one system. Job notes, photos, and the scope are somewhere else. Techs show up without the right part or the right info. Return trips eat your margin and test the customer's patience.
5
Handoff 05
Finished job to paid invoice
The job is done, but closing it out depends on someone moving it into QuickBooks. Change orders never get billed. Nobody asks for a review. This is money you already earned, leaking out the back door.
03 · Self-Check

Find your cracks this week

You don't need special software for this. You need about an hour, a few real jobs, and a notepad.

Step 1. Pick five jobs from last month

Choose a mix. Include at least one job you won, one you lost, and one that dragged on. The lost one will teach you the most.

Step 2. Trace each one start to finish

Start at the very first contact and end at the paid invoice. Write down every tool the job touched and every person who touched it.

Step 3. Mark the "someone had to remember" spots

Circle every step that only happened because a person remembered to do it. Someone retyped a lead. Someone called back. Someone moved the job into QuickBooks. Each circle is a crack.

Step 4. Look for the pattern

The same circle will show up across several jobs. That's your biggest leak. Fix that one first.

04 · The Fix

How to close the gaps

Give every handoff an owner

"The office handles it" isn't an owner. A name is. Each handoff needs one person who is responsible for it, even when a tool does most of the work.

Make the next step automatic

Anything that depends on memory will fail on a busy week. A missed call can trigger a text back right away. A new lead can get a booking link. An open estimate can get follow-up messages on a schedule. A closed job can send a review request on its own. Most field service tools can already do some of this. It just hasn't been turned on or set up right.

Connect your tools so data moves once

Every time someone retypes information from one system into another, a job can slip. Homeshow leads should flow straight into your CRM. Finished jobs should flow into accounting. Enter it once, and let it travel.

Watch one simple report

Track four numbers every week: leads in, estimates booked, jobs won, and jobs invoiced. Where the numbers drop the most is where your next crack is. A clean version of this report also tells you whether your fixes are working.

05 · Real Examples

What this looks like in real life

Coho Construction · Lead intake
$18K First project booked by the text agent
One owner was running admin, dispatch, and field work. Calls went unanswered while he was on job sites. We set up a text agent for missed calls that replied, answered questions, and booked the appointment. The first job it booked was an $18,000 project. He found out when it showed up on his calendar.
Home Services Company · Seven tools, none connected
40% Weekend homeshow leads lost before the fix
Homeshow leads went on paper and many never made it into the system. We replaced the paper with tablets and a booking link, and routed every lead into ServiceTitan automatically. Monday morning, the sales team was ready to call. They didn't buy a single new tool.
