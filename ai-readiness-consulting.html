The Real Problem

AI doesn't fix messy data. It repeats it faster.

Most small businesses and nonprofits don't have an AI problem. They have a foundation problem. Customer records live in three places. Donor data doesn't match the books. Nobody wrote down how the work actually gets done. Point an AI tool at that and you get confident answers built on bad inputs.

AI readiness is the work of getting the foundation right first: knowing what data you have, where it lives, whether it can be trusted, who should be allowed to see it, and which tasks are worth handing to AI at all. It's less exciting than a chatbot demo. It's also the difference between a tool your team uses every day and a subscription nobody opens after month two.

What We Assess

What AI readiness actually covers

Every assessment ends with a scored, plain-language answer to one question: can we use AI right now, and where?

Data Quality
  • Duplicate and conflicting records across tools
  • Missing fields AI would need to be useful
  • Which reports and sources can actually be trusted
Systems Connectivity
  • Whether your CRM, accounting, and project tools share data
  • Exports, APIs, and integration options in your current stack
  • Where manual copy-paste is holding data hostage
Process Clarity
  • Which workflows are documented vs. living in someone's head
  • Repeatable tasks that are good AI candidates
  • Tasks that should stay human, and why
Privacy, Access & Adoption
  • What customer, donor, or client data is safe to share with AI tools
  • Account settings, permissions, and a simple usage policy
  • Team readiness and the training needed to make it stick
Quick Self-Check

AI readiness checklist for SMBs and nonprofits

If you answer "no" or "not sure" to three or more of these, start with readiness before buying more AI tools.

  • We know which system is the source of truth for customers or donors.
  • Our CRM and accounting numbers agree without manual reconciliation.
  • Our most repeated workflows are written down somewhere.
  • We can export our core data cleanly from every tool we pay for.
  • We have a rule for what data can and can't go into AI tools.
  • Staff use the same naming, folders, and fields for the same things.
  • We can name three tasks that take hours each week and follow the same steps every time.
  • Someone owns AI decisions, even if it's only part of their job.

The Approach

How to prepare company data for AI

This is the sequence we follow with clients. You can do parts of it yourself; the audit makes it faster and puts a dollar value on each fix.

1
Step 01
Inventory the stack
List every tool, what data lives in it, who owns it, and how data moves between them. Most teams find at least one tool they're paying for and not using, and one critical spreadsheet nobody knew about.
2
Step 02
Pick a source of truth
For each core record (customer, donor, project, invoice) decide which system wins. Everything else syncs from it or gets retired.
3
Step 03
Clean what matters, archive the rest
Not all data deserves cleanup. We focus on the records AI will actually touch and set simple rules so it stays clean.
4
Step 04
Set guardrails
Decide what's safe to share with AI tools, configure the business or nonprofit versions of those tools, and write a one-page usage policy your team will read.
5
Step 05
Pilot one workflow
Pick one repeatable, measurable task, automate it with AI, and measure hours saved before expanding. Small wins build trust faster than big rollouts.

Questions

What people ask before engaging

What is AI readiness consulting?
AI readiness consulting assesses whether your data, systems, processes, and team are prepared to use AI tools reliably and safely. It identifies what would fail if you deployed AI today, what could work quickly with small fixes, and a prioritized plan to close the gaps.
Is my small business too small for AI readiness work?
If you have a team running three or more tools that don't share data, you're not too small. The work scales to your size. A ten-person team usually needs a cleanup and one well-chosen pilot, not an enterprise AI strategy.
Which AI tool should we use: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, or Gemini?
It depends on where your data already lives and what you need it to do. Microsoft 365 shops often start with Copilot, Google Workspace shops with Gemini, and teams that want flexible writing, analysis, and agent workflows often choose Claude or ChatGPT. We recommend based on your stack, not on a vendor relationship.
Is it safe to put customer or donor data into AI tools?
It can be, with the right account type, settings, and rules. Part of readiness is deciding what data is safe to share, configuring business or nonprofit versions of AI tools that don't train on your data, and writing a short usage policy your staff can follow.
Do you work remotely?
Yes. AI readiness engagements are remote by default for clients anywhere in the U.S. or North America. We also work hybrid and in person across the Seattle and Tacoma metros (King, Pierce, and Snohomish Counties, including Sammamish) for in-person workshops and process mapping when that helps.
